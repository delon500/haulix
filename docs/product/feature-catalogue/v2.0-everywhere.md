# Feature Catalogue — v2.0 Everywhere

v2.0 makes the app run on any device and be controlled from anywhere: 25 features (74–98). The engine can now live on a home server or NAS, users manage it from a browser or phone, and it adapts to expensive data, power cuts and busy networks. The format, personas and tags are the same as the earlier files.

## Run anywhere, control from anywhere

These five features separate where downloading happens from where users control it, which is the main idea behind the engine-plus-clients architecture.

### 74. Headless/server mode (Docker, NAS, Raspberry Pi, VPS)

**Multi-device and remote · v2.0 · Free**

**What it is:** The download engine can run on its own, without a screen or the desktop app, on an always-on device: a home server, a NAS (network storage box), a Raspberry Pi or a rented cloud server. Users control it from the web dashboard, phone app or desktop app on another computer.

**User story:** As Sam, I want the engine running on my NAS around the clock, so that downloads continue even when my laptop is closed or travelling with me.

**Scenario:** Sam installs the Docker image on his Synology NAS with one command. It saves straight to the NAS's media folder. From then on his laptop, phone and browser all send downloads to the NAS, which works through them day and night.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Official Docker image for x86 and ARM; native packages for Synology and Unraid; install guides for Raspberry Pi OS and common Linux servers.
- Runs with no screen, configured through the web dashboard or a settings file.
- Uses under 150 MB of memory when idle, so it runs comfortably on small devices.
- Remote access needs a password or device pairing; it is never open to the internet by default.
- All download features (media, rules, subscriptions, schedules) work the same as on the desktop.

### 75. Web dashboard

**Multi-device and remote · v2.0 · Pro**

**What it is:** The full app interface, opened in any web browser, served by the engine itself. It's the same interface as the desktop app, so users can manage downloads from a work computer, tablet or Chromebook without installing anything.

**User story:** As Priya, I want to manage my home downloads from my locked-down work laptop's browser, so that I can queue things without installing software I'm not allowed to install.

**Scenario:** At the office, Priya opens her dashboard address in Edge, signs in, and adds three links to her home queue. She checks back at lunch and all three are done.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Offers the same features and layout as the desktop app, in current versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari.
- Works on screens from phone size to desktop.
- Access is protected by login, with optional two-factor authentication.
- Reachable from outside the home network through the encrypted relay, without the user setting up port forwarding.
- Updates live: progress refreshes in real time without reloading the page.

### 76. Full mobile app (queue, library, streaming)

**Multi-device and remote · v2.0 · Pro**

**What it is:** The phone companion from v1.0 grows into a full app. Users can manage queues and rules, browse their whole library, and stream downloaded videos and audio from their home computer to their phone.

**User story:** As Maya, I want to watch lectures stored on my home computer from my phone, so that I can study on the bus without copying files to my phone first.

**Scenario:** On the bus, Maya opens the app, browses to her "Biology" collection, and taps a lecture. It streams from her home PC over the encrypted relay, starting where she stopped last night.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Manages queues, rules, subscriptions and settings from the phone.
- Browses the full library with search, tags, collections and watched status.
- Streams video and audio from the home computer, adjusting quality to the phone's connection.
- Files can be saved to the phone for offline viewing.
- Streaming is encrypted end to end, and respects metered mode (features 81–86) on mobile data.

### 77. Synced queue, history and settings

**Multi-device and remote · v2.0 · Pro**

**What it is:** A user's queues, history, rules, subscriptions and settings stay the same on every computer and device they use. Change a rule on the laptop and it's already changed on the desktop.

**User story:** As Diego, I want my rules and settings the same on my editing PC and my laptop, so that I don't have to set everything up twice or keep them matched by hand.

**Scenario:** Diego creates a new naming template on his editing PC. When he opens the app on his laptop that evening, the template is already there, and his history shows downloads from both machines.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Syncs rules, subscriptions, settings, tags, collections, history and watched progress.
- Changes reach other devices within 10 seconds when they're online.
- Edits made offline sync when the device reconnects; conflicts are resolved automatically, with the newest change winning per item.
- All synced data is encrypted end to end (feature 64).
- Users can choose what syncs, and can turn sync off for any device.

### 78. Download to cloud storage (Google Drive, OneDrive, S3, Dropbox)

**Multi-device and remote · v2.0 · Pro**

**What it is:** Finished downloads can be uploaded automatically to the user's own cloud storage account, as the save location or as a post-download action.

**User story:** As Diego, I want client footage uploaded to our shared Google Drive as soon as it finishes, so that my editor can start on it without me moving files.

**Scenario:** Diego sets his "Client Acme" queue to upload finished files to a shared Drive folder. Overnight, 12 clips download and upload. His editor in another city finds them in the folder the next morning.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Supports Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox and S3-compatible storage.
- Connection uses each service's official sign-in; the app never sees the user's password.
- Upload can be the destination (with the local copy removed after) or an extra copy.
- Uploads resume after interruptions and show progress like downloads.
- Can be set per queue, per download or through rules.

## Sharing and self-hosting

These two features let households share downloads efficiently, and let privacy-minded users run everything without our servers.

### 79. LAN sharing: download once, share locally ★

**Sharing and collaboration · v2.0 · Pro · ★ Differentiator**

**What it is:** When one device on a home or office network has already downloaded a file, the other devices on that network get it directly from that device, over the local network, instead of downloading it again from the internet. The devices find each other automatically.

**User story:** As a parent in a family of four, I want a file downloaded once at home to be available to every device in the house, so that we don't use our data allowance four times for the same thing.

**Scenario:** Priya downloads a 6 GB game update on the family PC. When her son's laptop asks for the same update, the app finds it on the family PC and copies it across the home Wi-Fi in two minutes, using no internet data.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Devices running the app on the same network discover each other automatically within 10 seconds.
- Before downloading from the internet, the app checks nearby devices for the same file (matched by checksum or source link).
- Local transfers are encrypted, and only happen between devices linked to the same account or approved household.
- Each device can choose what it shares, or turn sharing off.
- The download view shows how much internet data was saved by local sharing.

### 80. Self-hosting option

**Security and privacy · v2.0 · Free**

**What it is:** Users who don't want to rely on our servers can run their own copy of the relay and sync service, on their own server. Their phone, desktop and web dashboard then connect only to that server.

**User story:** As Sam, I want to run the relay and sync service on my own server, so that none of my data depends on, or passes through, a company's infrastructure.

**Scenario:** Sam deploys the self-hosted relay as a Docker container next to his engine. In each app he enters his own server address instead of the default. Send-from-phone, sync and remote access all work, and nothing touches our servers.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- The relay and sync server is available as a Docker image with documented setup.
- Apps can be pointed to a custom server address during pairing or in settings.
- Self-hosted setups get all remote features (relay, sync, push) without a Pro subscription for remote use.
- The self-hosted server code is open source.
- Self-hosting documentation covers backups, updates and securing the server.

## Metered and data-aware mode

These six features protect users on expensive or limited connections: phone hotspots, mobile broadband, satellite internet, hotel Wi-Fi, and capped home plans common across much of the world.

### 81. Metered connection detection ★

**Metered and data-aware mode · v2.0 · Free · ★ Differentiator**

**What it is:** The app recognises when the computer is on a metered connection, such as a phone hotspot or mobile broadband, and switches into a careful mode automatically. It uses the operating system's own metered setting, plus its own checks.

**User story:** As Priya, I want the app to notice when I'm on my phone's hotspot, so that it doesn't burn through my mobile data with background downloads.

**Scenario:** Priya's laptop joins her phone's hotspot on a train. The app shows a small "Metered connection" badge, pauses her large background queue, and lets only the small file she's actively waiting for continue.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Detects connections marked as metered in Windows, macOS and Linux settings, and phone hotspots where the system reports them.
- Users can mark any Wi-Fi network as metered or unmetered, and the app remembers it.
- A clear badge shows when metered mode is on, with a one-click override.
- What happens on metered connections is configurable (pause all, pause large files, continue with limits).
- Switching back to an unmetered network resumes paused downloads automatically.

### 82. Size and cost preview in the user's currency ★

**Metered and data-aware mode · v2.0 · Free · ★ Differentiator**

**What it is:** Before downloading, the app shows the file size and what it will cost in money on the current connection, based on the user's own data price (for example, "1.4 GB · about €7 on mobile data · free on home Wi-Fi"). No other download manager does this.

**User story:** As Priya, I want to see what a download will cost before I start it, so that I can decide whether to wait until I'm on cheaper internet.

**Scenario:** On a hotel connection that charges by the gigabyte, Priya pastes a course video link. The app shows "2.1 GB · about $10 on this network". She picks 720p instead, which shows "640 MB · about $3", and starts it.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Users enter a data price per network or connection type, in any currency, in a simple setup.
- The preview shows size and estimated cost, alongside each quality option.
- Unknown sizes show as "size unknown" rather than a guess.
- Costs use the user's chosen currency and local number format.
- The feature is optional and hidden for users who don't set a price.

### 83. Daily, weekly and monthly data budgets

**Metered and data-aware mode · v2.0 · Pro**

**What it is:** Users set how much data the app may use per day, week or month, overall or per connection. When a budget is nearly used up, the app warns them; when it's used up, downloads pause until it resets.

**User story:** As Priya, I want to cap the app at 10 GB per month on my mobile broadband, so that I never get an overage bill.

**Scenario:** Priya sets a 10 GB monthly budget on her mobile broadband. On the 24th, the app warns "8 GB of 10 GB used". When she reaches 10 GB, downloads on that connection pause until the 1st, while downloads on Wi-Fi continue.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Budgets can be set per day, week or month, globally or per network, with a chosen reset day.
- Warnings at 80% and 100% of a budget, by notification.
- When a budget runs out, downloads on that connection pause and resume automatically when it resets.
- The user can raise or override a budget at any time.
- Data use is measured by the app accurately to within 2%.

### 84. Wait-for-Wi-Fi and off-peak rules

**Metered and data-aware mode · v2.0 · Pro**

**What it is:** Downloads can be told to wait until a cheaper connection is available: until the computer is on Wi-Fi, a particular network, or inside an off-peak time window when the user's provider charges less.

**User story:** As Maya, I want large downloads to wait until my provider's cheap night-time data starts, so that a semester's lectures don't use my expensive daytime data.

**Scenario:** Maya's mobile plan includes cheap data from midnight to 6 am. She sets a rule: "Files over 200 MB wait for off-peak." A playlist she queues at 7 pm downloads entirely after midnight.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Rules can wait for: any unmetered network, a named network, or a time window, or a combination.
- Waiting downloads show the reason clearly ("Waiting for Wi-Fi").
- Size thresholds decide which downloads wait (for example, files over 200 MB).
- Users can start any waiting download immediately with one click.

### 85. Lower quality automatically on metered connections

**Metered and data-aware mode · v2.0 · Pro**

**What it is:** On metered connections, the app picks a lower video quality by default (for example, 480p instead of 1080p), cutting data use while still downloading what the user asked for.

**User story:** As Priya, I want videos downloaded at lower quality on mobile data, so that I can still save talks while travelling without using much data.

**Scenario:** Priya sends a 1-hour talk to download while on her hotspot. The app saves it at 480p (about 250 MB) instead of her usual 1080p (about 1.5 GB), and notes this on the download.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Users choose the default quality for metered connections separately from unmetered ones.
- Downloads using the lower setting are labelled, with an option to re-download in full quality later on Wi-Fi.
- Choosing a quality manually always overrides the automatic setting.
- Applies to audio quality as well as video.

### 86. Data usage reports

**Metered and data-aware mode · v2.0 · Pro**

**What it is:** Charts showing how much data the app has used over time, broken down by network, site, category or (for shared households) user, so people can see where their data goes.

**User story:** As Sam, I want to see which sites and networks use the most data, so that I can adjust rules and budgets with real numbers.

**Scenario:** Sam opens the usage report at the end of the month. It shows 340 GB total, 70% from two video sites, and 4 GB on his phone hotspot, which he didn't expect. He adds a rule to block large downloads on the hotspot.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Shows data used per day, week and month in a chart.
- Breaks down usage by network, site, category, queue and (with household sharing) device or user.
- Shows data saved by LAN sharing (feature 79) and by lower quality on metered connections.
- Reports can be exported as CSV.
- All usage data stays on the user's devices.

## Power and network intelligence

These six features let the app make smart decisions on its own: sharing bandwidth fairly, reacting to where the computer is connected, and planning around battery and power cuts.

### 87. Polite mode (auto-throttle when the network is busy) ★

**Network intelligence · v2.0 · Pro · ★ Differentiator**

**What it is:** The app watches how responsive the network is. When someone else starts using it heavily (a video call, an online game, streaming), the app slows its downloads automatically, then speeds back up when the network is free. Users never have to set a speed limit.

**User story:** As a parent sharing home internet, I want big downloads to step aside automatically when someone is on a video call or gaming, so that nobody in the house complains about lag.

**Scenario:** Diego is downloading 50 GB at full speed. His partner starts a video call in the next room. Within seconds, the app notices the network slowing and drops to 20% of the connection. When the call ends, it climbs back to full speed.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Measures network delay continuously and reduces speed when delay rises above normal levels.
- Reacts within 3 seconds of other traffic starting, and returns to full speed within 30 seconds of it ending.
- Keeps added delay for other users under 50 ms in testing with video calls and games.
- Can be turned on globally or per queue, and shows when it is slowing downloads.
- Works without any router setup.

### 88. Per-network rules

**Network intelligence · v2.0 · Pro**

**What it is:** Users can set different behaviour for different networks: pause everything on hotel Wi-Fi, use the proxy only at the office, run unlimited at home. The app applies the right settings automatically when the computer joins each network.

**User story:** As Priya, I want the app to behave differently at home, at the office and on public Wi-Fi, so that I don't have to change settings every time I move.

**Scenario:** Priya sets: home Wi-Fi → no limits; office → use company proxy, cap at 5 MB/s; any public Wi-Fi → pause large downloads. Her laptop applies the right setting each time it connects.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Networks are recognised by name and, where available, by router identity, so look-alike networks aren't confused.
- Per-network settings: speed limits, proxy, metered on/off, which queues run, and pause all.
- A default setting applies to unknown networks, such as "treat as public".
- The current network and active rule are shown in the status bar.

### 89. Power-aware scheduling (battery, UPS)

**Power and resilience · v2.0 · Pro**

**What it is:** The app plans downloads around power. On battery, it can slow down, pause large downloads, or only run at a certain charge level. When plugged in, it runs normally. It can also read the status of a UPS (backup battery box) during power cuts.

**User story:** As Priya, I want large downloads to pause when my laptop is on battery, so that I don't arrive at a meeting with a flat laptop.

**Scenario:** Priya unplugs her laptop to move to a meeting room. The app pauses her large background downloads and keeps only an active small one running. When she plugs back in an hour later, everything resumes.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Settings for on battery: continue, pause large downloads, pause all, or pause below a chosen charge level.
- Reads battery status on Windows, macOS and Linux laptops.
- Reads UPS status where the system reports it (for example, via NUT on home servers).
- Resumes automatically when power is restored or the battery recovers above the threshold.

### 90. Outage schedule plugins (for example, load shedding) ★

**Power and resilience · v2.0 · Pro · ★ Differentiator**

**What it is:** In countries with scheduled power cuts (such as load shedding in South Africa, and planned outages in Pakistan, Nigeria and elsewhere), a plugin reads the user's local outage schedule. The app then starts big downloads right after power returns, warns when a download won't finish in time, and pauses safely before a cut.

**User story:** As someone living with scheduled power cuts, I want downloads planned around my outage schedule, so that large files finish between cuts instead of failing halfway.

**Scenario:** Priya is working from Johannesburg, where load shedding is scheduled from 18:00 to 20:30. At 17:00 the app warns: "This 20 GB download won't finish before the 18:00 outage." It pauses safely at 17:55 and resumes automatically when power returns at 20:30.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Schedules come from plugins, so each country or service can have its own (starting with a South African load shedding plugin).
- Users choose their area once; the schedule updates automatically.
- Warnings when an active download won't finish before the next outage.
- Safe pause 5 minutes before a scheduled outage (adjustable), with resume after power returns.
- Plugins are sandboxed and can only provide schedule data.

### 91. Safe pause before low battery or a planned outage

**Power and resilience · v2.0 · Pro**

**What it is:** Before the computer runs out of battery or loses power at a known time, the app pauses all downloads cleanly and saves everything, so nothing is left half-written when the power goes.

**User story:** As Diego, I want downloads paused cleanly before my battery dies, so that I never have to check files for damage after the laptop shuts off.

**Scenario:** Diego's laptop drops to 6% battery while he's away from his desk. The app pauses all downloads and saves their state. When he plugs in and restarts, everything resumes without errors.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Pauses and saves all downloads at a chosen battery level (default 7%).
- Pauses and saves before scheduled outages (feature 90) and when a UPS reports low battery.
- Completes the safe pause within 5 seconds.
- Shows a notification explaining why downloads paused and when they will resume.

### 92. Mirror and CDN failover mid-download

**Core download engine · v2.0 · Free**

**What it is:** Many large files are available from several servers (mirrors). If the server a download is using becomes slow or stops responding, the app continues the same file from another mirror, without starting again.

**User story:** As Sam, I want downloads to switch servers automatically when one stalls, so that a bad mirror doesn't leave a large download stuck at 60%.

**Scenario:** Sam is downloading a 5 GB ISO from a university mirror, which slows to a crawl at 60%. The app detects the slowdown, switches the remaining segments to two faster mirrors listed on the project's site, and finishes in a few minutes.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Accepts several mirror links for one file, from the user, a Metalink file, or a site's mirror list.
- Switches a segment to another mirror when the current one stalls (no data for 15 seconds) or falls below 20% of its earlier speed.
- Can download different segments from different mirrors at once.
- Verifies the finished file with a checksum when available, so mixing mirrors can't corrupt it.
- Shows which mirrors were used in the download details.

## More sources

These three features widen what the app can download: torrents, live streams and image galleries.

### 93. BitTorrent, magnet links and Metalink

**Core download engine · v2.0 · Free**

**What it is:** The app can download using BitTorrent (where a file is shared between many people's computers) from .torrent files and magnet links, plus Metalink files, which list many mirrors for one download. Many large open-source projects and public datasets are distributed this way.

**User story:** As Sam, I want to download Linux distributions and public datasets from torrents in the same app, so that I don't need a separate torrent client.

**Scenario:** Sam clicks a magnet link for a Linux distribution on its official site. The app opens it, shows the files inside, and downloads it alongside his other downloads in the same queue.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Opens .torrent files and magnet links, and can be set as the system's handler for them.
- Users can choose which files inside a torrent to download.
- Upload (sharing back) speed and ratio limits can be set, with a stop-sharing option when done.
- Torrents respect metered mode, budgets, schedules and per-network rules like any other download.
- Metalink files are supported, using all listed mirrors and verifying checksums.

### 94. Livestream recording, including scheduled

**Media extraction · v2.0 · Pro**

**What it is:** The app can record a live stream as it happens, from the start of the broadcast if the site allows, and can be set to start recording automatically when a scheduled stream begins.

**User story:** As Maya, I want a live online lecture recorded automatically when it starts, so that I can watch it later when it clashes with my part-time job.

**Scenario:** Maya's professor streams a guest lecture at 14:00 while she's at work. She schedules a recording the night before. The app starts recording at 14:00, stops when the stream ends, and the file is waiting in her Lectures folder.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Records live streams on supported sites, from the current moment or the start of the stream where possible.
- Recordings can be scheduled for a date and time, or set to start when a followed channel goes live.
- Recording stops when the stream ends, or at a chosen time or size limit.
- Survives brief connection drops by reconnecting and continuing, noting any gap.
- Free users get a limited version; the exact limits are set with pricing before v1.0.

### 95. Image gallery downloads (gallery-dl)

**Media extraction · v2.0 · Free**

**What it is:** Download whole image galleries, albums and profiles from image-heavy sites, using gallery-dl, a tool built for this job just as yt-dlp is for video.

**User story:** As Diego, I want to save a whole reference gallery in one step, so that I have every image for a mood board instead of saving them one by one.

**Scenario:** Diego pastes the link to a 240-image reference album. The app lists all 240 images with thumbnails, he deselects 30, and the rest download into a folder named after the album.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Supports the image sites gallery-dl supports, through the same paste-a-link flow as videos.
- Shows a thumbnail grid before downloading, with select all, select none and individual selection.
- Original resolution is downloaded by default, with filenames and folders following the user's templates.
- gallery-dl is auto-updated in the background, like yt-dlp (feature 10).
- Respects site login (feature 4) for galleries that need it.

## Notifications and cloud add-on

These three features keep users informed across all their devices, and add a paid option for people with no always-on computer at home.

### 96. Completion, failure and budget alerts

**Notifications · v2.0 · Free**

**What it is:** One set of alerts that works across desktop, phone and web: downloads finished or failed, data budgets nearly used up, an outage approaching, or a server engine going offline. Users choose which alerts go to which device.

**User story:** As Sam, I want an alert on my phone if my home server stops working, so that I can fix it before a night's worth of scheduled downloads is lost.

**Scenario:** Sam's NAS loses power during a storm. Ten minutes later his phone shows: "Home server is offline. 14 downloads paused." When power returns, a second alert confirms everything resumed.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Alert types: finished, failed, needs input, budget warnings (feature 83), upcoming outage (feature 90), engine offline or back online.
- Each alert type can be sent to desktop, phone, web dashboard, or turned off.
- An engine that hasn't reported in for 10 minutes triggers an offline alert on the user's other devices.
- Alerts never duplicate: one event shows once per device.

### 97. Daily digest of subscription downloads

**Notifications · v2.0 · Pro**

**What it is:** Instead of a notification for every new episode, users get one summary a day (or week) of everything their subscriptions downloaded, with links to watch or listen.

**User story:** As Priya, I want one morning summary of new episodes from my subscriptions, so that I'm not interrupted by a notification every time something downloads overnight.

**Scenario:** At 7:00 Priya gets one notification: "7 new items overnight: 3 podcast episodes, 4 videos from 2 channels." She taps it to see the list and picks two for her commute.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Daily or weekly digest at a time the user chooses.
- Groups items by subscription, with titles, lengths and thumbnails.
- Tapping an item opens it in the player on that device, or streams it from home.
- Individual per-item notifications for subscriptions are turned off when the digest is on, unless the user keeps them.

### 98. Cloud add-on (remote downloading and storage)

**Monetization · v2.0 · Business model**

**What it is:** A paid add-on for users who don't have an always-on computer at home. We run the engine for them in the cloud: they send links from any device, our server downloads them at high speed, and they collect the files from cloud storage when convenient.

**User story:** As Maya, I don't have a computer that stays on at home, but I want to send links from my phone and have them downloaded somewhere, so that the files are ready to collect over campus Wi-Fi.

**Scenario:** Maya subscribes to the cloud add-on. On a weekend away, she sends 5 lecture links from her phone. Our server downloads them in minutes. On Monday, on campus Wi-Fi, she downloads them from her cloud space to her laptop.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Each subscriber gets a cloud engine with a storage allowance (for example 100 GB), with plans for more.
- Works with send-from-phone, the web dashboard, rules and subscriptions, like a home engine.
- Files can be downloaded to devices, streamed, or sent to the user's own cloud storage (feature 78).
- Files are kept for a set period (for example 30 days) and users are warned before deletion.
- Clear acceptable-use rules and a takedown process are in place before launch, because downloads happen on our servers.
- Priced separately from Pro, and also available to lifetime licence owners (feature 70).
