# Feature Catalogue — v3.0 Intelligence

v3.0 turns the download library into a personal knowledge base: 7 features (F-099–F-105). It builds on v1.0's transcripts and search with summaries, meaning-based search, automatic tagging and plain-English commands. AI runs locally by default for privacy; a cloud option is offered to Pro users who want faster or higher-quality results.

## Search and AI

These six features help users understand, find and control what they've downloaded, without reading or watching all of it.

### F-099 AI summaries, chapters and key points

**Search and AI · v3.0 · Pro**

**What it is:** For any transcribed video or audio, or any downloaded document, the app writes a short summary, splits long content into labelled chapters, and lists the key points. It works from the transcript or text, so every point links back to where it came from.

**User story:** As Maya, I want a summary and key points for each lecture, so that I can revise a whole semester quickly before exams.

**Scenario:** The night before an exam, Maya opens 12 lectures one by one and reads each 5-line summary and key points list. For one point she doesn't understand, she clicks it and the video jumps to that explanation.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Generates a summary (about 100 words), chapters with titles and timestamps, and 5–10 key points.
- Every chapter and key point links to its timestamp or page in the source.
- Runs locally by default; users can choose a cloud AI model instead, with a clear explanation that text is sent to that provider.
- Summaries are labelled as AI-generated, and users can regenerate or edit them.
- Works in the same languages as transcription (feature F-059).

### F-100 Semantic search

**Search and AI · v3.0 · Pro**

**What it is:** Search by meaning, not just exact words. A search for "the video where someone explained how containers work" finds the right lecture even if it never used those words.

**User story:** As Diego, I want to find clips by describing what happens in them, so that I don't need to remember the exact words anyone said.

**Scenario:** Diego searches "someone talking about learning to edit on an old laptop". The top result is an interview where the speaker says "I taught myself on a hand-me-down computer", which a word search would have missed.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Search combines meaning-based results with exact word matches, ranked together.
- Works across transcripts, documents and summaries.
- The meaning index is built and stored locally, updating as files are added.
- Results appear within 1 second for a library of 1,000 hours of transcripts.
- Each result shows why it matched, with the relevant passage.

### F-101 Auto-tagging and categorization

**Search and AI · v3.0 · Pro**

**What it is:** The app suggests tags and categories for new downloads based on their content, such as "Biology", "Interview" or "Tutorial", so the library organises itself without the user tagging everything by hand.

**User story:** As Maya, I want new downloads tagged by subject automatically, so that my library stays organised without me tagging every file.

**Scenario:** Maya downloads 6 new videos. The app suggests tags for each: three as "Organic Chemistry", two as "Exam prep" and one as "Career talk". She accepts all with one click.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Suggests up to 5 tags per item from its content, preferring tags the user already uses.
- Users can accept all, accept some, or reject suggestions; nothing is applied silently unless the user turns on auto-apply.
- Learns from accepted and rejected suggestions over time.
- Runs locally by default, like other AI features.

### F-102 Natural-language commands

**Search and AI · v3.0 · Pro**

**What it is:** Users can type what they want in everyday words, such as "download this playlist as audio for my run" or "pause everything until tonight", and the app works out the right settings and does it, showing what it will do first.

**User story:** As Priya, I want to tell the app what I need in plain words, so that I don't have to find the right menus and settings.

**Scenario:** Priya pastes a playlist link and types "audio only, small files, put them in my Running folder, start after midnight". The app shows: "Audio only (Opus, low quality) · Folder: Running · Starts at 00:00 · 24 items, about 300 MB". She clicks Confirm.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Understands requests about downloading, formats, folders, scheduling, queues, rules and searching.
- Always shows a summary of what it will do, which the user confirms or edits before anything happens.
- Asks a short question when a request is unclear, instead of guessing.
- Can create rules from descriptions ("always save videos from this site as 720p").
- Available in the command palette (feature F-055) and on mobile.

### F-103 Text extraction and search in PDFs (including OCR)

**Search and AI · v3.0 · Pro**

**What it is:** The app reads text from documents, including scanned PDFs and images of text, using OCR (optical character recognition, which turns pictures of text into real text). That text becomes searchable alongside transcripts.

**User story:** As Maya, I want scanned lecture handouts to be searchable, so that I can find a formula or definition without opening every PDF.

**Scenario:** Maya's professor shares scanned handouts as image-only PDFs. The app reads them overnight. Next day, Maya searches "Michaelis-Menten" and finds it on page 4 of a handout from week 3.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Extracts text from PDFs, images (PNG, JPG) and scanned documents, in at least 20 languages.
- Runs locally, in the background, at low priority.
- Search results open the document at the right page, with the matching words highlighted.
- Users can export extracted text, or save a searchable copy of the PDF.

### F-104 Subtitle generation and translation

**Search and AI · v3.0 · Pro**

**What it is:** The app creates subtitles for videos that don't have any, from their transcript, and can translate subtitles into another language, so users can watch content in languages they don't speak.

**User story:** As Diego, I want subtitles translated into English for foreign-language reference videos, so that I can understand them and use them in my work.

**Scenario:** Diego downloads a Japanese documentary with no subtitles. The app transcribes it, generates Japanese subtitles, and translates them into English. He watches it with English subtitles in the built-in player.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Generates subtitles from transcripts, timed to the speech within 0.5 seconds.
- Translates subtitles between at least 20 languages.
- Subtitles can be embedded in the video or saved as separate .srt or .vtt files.
- Translated subtitles are labelled as machine-translated.
- Local translation by default, with an optional cloud model for higher quality.

## Browser integration

This feature completes browser coverage by adding Safari, the default browser on every Mac.

### F-105 Safari extension

**Browser integration · v3.0 · Free**

**What it is:** The browser extension becomes available for Safari on macOS, with the same features as the other browsers. Safari extensions are packaged differently, as a small Mac app distributed through the Mac App Store, which is why it comes later than the others.

**User story:** As Maya, I want the extension in Safari, my Mac's default browser, so that I don't have to switch browsers just to send downloads to the app.

**Scenario:** Maya enables the extension in Safari's settings after installing it from the Mac App Store. The download button now appears on lecture videos in Safari, and links go straight to the app.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- Published in the Mac App Store and enabled from Safari's Extensions settings.
- Offers the same features as the Chrome version: interception, media button, right-click options and login passthrough.
- First-run setup on macOS detects Safari and links to the App Store listing.
- Meets Apple's App Store review rules, including not downloading media itself (it hands links to the app, as the other extensions do).
